## 1. Background & rationale

`rules/2026-01` is the concrete rules-domain instance registered for the active CERTCRYPT public architecture. Only this registry entry defines the active public rules domain.

This registry entry exists to keep the generic `rules-domain/1/specification.md` free of any concrete parameter values for any specific rules domain. Concrete parameters belong here so that:

- the generic specification remains stable across the lifecycle of any individual rules-domain instance
- new rules-domain instances can be registered by adding a registry entry, without modifying the generic specification
- there is a single canonical source of truth for the parameters of `rules/2026-01`, eliminating duplication

This registry entry defines:

- one exact rules-domain identifier
- one exact system-domain binding for that identifier
- one exact fixation service
- one exact permitted settlement profile and its parameters
- one exact settlement-reference resolution method
- one exact finality profile
- one exact settlement-material contract
- one exact capacity-consumption interpretation
- one exact authorization-to-consumption bridge
- one exact leaf-assignment interpretation
- one exact capacity-commitment TTL
- one exact set of close-window profiles
- one exact set of admitted subject profiles
- one exact signature profile for each signed artifact

It does **not** define:

- generic rules-domain mechanics
- generic settlement-profile completeness rules
- generic equivalence or successor rules
- the canonical bootstrap statement under which the system domain is published
- the system-manifest model

Those concerns belong to other specifications.

## 5. System-domain binding

`rules/2026-01` is registered under the `certcrypt` system domain, whose identity
and bootstrap are declared in [its registry entry](../index.md). This entry does
not restate them: a parameter carried in two normative files is a parameter that
can diverge, and the one that governs attribution is the domain's, not this one's.

Accordingly, `rules/2026-01` belongs to that system domain if and only if the
artifact resolves to the bootstrap declared there — `system_id`,
`foundation_profile_id`, `foundation_statement_id` and
`bootstrap_system_root_fingerprint` are read from that entry, and the resolution
order is [system-domain/1](../../../specification.md) Section 16.1.

A rotation of the root manifest under `system-manifest/1` does not change this
binding: every artifact under `rules/2026-01` carries the bootstrap fingerprint,
and the digest of a later manifest version is never accepted in its place
(`system-manifest/1` Section 14).

No verifier MAY treat a publication transaction id, account-creation
transaction, account name, or other publication-layer locator as normatively
equivalent to the required system-domain attribution unless public rules define
that equivalence explicitly.

## 6. Fixation service

For `rules/2026-01`, the party that performs fixation — the recipient of a
submission set, and the only party that accepts or rejects its members
(`submission-set/1` Section 6) — is:

- `fixation_service = "certcrypt"`

`submission-set/1` states that party's obligations without naming it, because
which party holds the role is a property of the rules domain rather than of the
protocol. This entry is where the role is filled, and it is filled by the same
system domain that Section 5 binds: no other party may accept members under
`rules/2026-01`.

A verifier does not evaluate this parameter. Fixation is not an artifact a
verifier inspects, and a valid fixation does not by itself produce `Independent`
— that requires settlement with sufficient finality (Section 10). The parameter
exists so that a reader of `submission-set/1` can resolve who its requirements
bind under this rules domain.

## 8. Settlement parameters

For `rules/2026-01`, the exact parameters selecting `settlement.aggregated.xrpl/1` are:

- `xrpl_chain_id = "xrpl-network-0"`
- `xrpl_mpt_issuance_id = "066AE75B79EAAC53741F55155550B40213F220B1EFD0F357"`
- `xrpl_settlement_account = "rEdGFQHY5VhadmVSHhxqWHNFVvfUw9XpMU"`
- `xrpl_action_name = "payment"`
- `xrpl_token_symbol = "CERT"`
- `xrpl_asset_scale = 2`
- `xrpl_memo_prefix = "ccab1:"`
- `root_hash_alg = "sha3-256-merkle-v1"`
- `leaf_hash_alg = "sha3-256-json-v1"`

All values are normative. Implementations MUST NOT substitute, reinterpret, or default any of these values.

### 8.1 Chain identity

`xrpl_chain_id` is the binding identifier of `rules/2026-01` to the XRP Ledger mainnet. XRPL defines no chain id: it reports a `network_id` (`0` mainnet, `1` testnet) and publishes nothing equivalent to a genesis hash. This rules-domain instance therefore binds a namespaced identity string derived from the network id.

`xrpl_chain_id` is an **identity string**. A verifier MUST compare it for exact equality and MUST NOT parse, decompose, or derive meaning from its internal structure. Its only normative function is to differ between networks, so that material settled on a non-mainnet network can never be accepted under `rules/2026-01`.

Any change to this identifier is not part of `rules/2026-01` and would constitute a successor rules-domain instance under [rules-domain/1/specification.md](../../../../../rules-domain/1/specification.md) Section 9.

### 8.2 Token identity and settlement mechanism

The `CERT` capacity token is an XRP Ledger **Multi-Purpose Token (MPT)**. `xrpl_mpt_issuance_id` is the exact MPT issuance identifier; it occupies the position that a token-contract account name occupies on an account-contract substrate. XRPL has no contract accounts, and no token-contract parameter exists under `rules/2026-01`.

The settlement destroys `CERT` supply by paying the MPT back to its own issuance. On XRPL, returning an MPT to its issuer removes that amount from circulation; that is how supply ceases to exist on this substrate. `xrpl_action_name = "payment"` is therefore the XRPL **transaction type**, not an action name on a contract.

There is no sink or destination parameter under `rules/2026-01`. The settlement has no third-party destination: the amount returns to the issuance itself. On XRPL that return is a payment whose destination is the issuance's issuer account, and that account is not a separate parameter: an MPT issuance identifier encodes the account that created the issuance, so the destination is fully determined by `xrpl_mpt_issuance_id`.

`xrpl_asset_scale` is the `AssetScale` of that issuance — the number of decimal places of one `CERT` unit — fixed on the ledger when the issuance was created and immutable since. A settled quantity is expressed on the ledger as an integer equal to the declared decimal quantity multiplied by `10^xrpl_asset_scale`; a quantity with more decimal places than the scale cannot be settled and MUST NOT be rounded.

`xrpl_settlement_account` is the account the settlement **debits** — the aggregate settlement account, never an issuer's own account. It is the *origin* of the burn, not a destination. This parameter is normatively required: every settlement within a window originates from the same aggregate account, so an observer of the ledger sees one aggregate burn and MUST NOT be able to attribute it to any individual issuer. A settlement whose originating account is not exactly `xrpl_settlement_account` is non-conformant.

A verifier MUST fail closed when `xrpl_settlement_account` is unset or empty. An unset account is not an absence of constraint; it is a configuration that cannot express one, and accepting under it would admit a settlement debited from an issuer's own account — precisely what this parameter exists to prevent. An implementation MUST NOT treat an unset value as "unconstrained" and MUST NOT skip the check.

`xrpl_mpt_issuance_id`, `xrpl_settlement_account` and `xrpl_asset_scale` are fixed values of objects that exist on the XRP Ledger mainnet, as `bootstrap_system_root_fingerprint` is a fixed value of the system domain's registry entry (Section 5). They are not defaults: an implementation MUST NOT substitute any other value.

`xrpl_memo_prefix = "ccab1:"` is a stable namespace identifier of the settlement memo format. It is not version-tied to the rules-domain identifier; it changes only when the settlement memo format itself changes.

`root_hash_alg` and `leaf_hash_alg` are substrate-independent: they name the Merkle and leaf digest constructions, which are unchanged by the settlement substrate. They carry no substrate prefix.

> **Note on abstraction levels.** `xrpl_action_name` records the substrate transaction type, not the protocol-level effect. The two are deliberately kept apart: Section 12 defines *capacity consumption* — what must have happened for capacity to be consumed — without naming a transaction, while this section fixes the exact value a verifier compares against `settlement_action.name`. `payment` is the XRPL transaction type that achieves the effect: paying an MPT back to its own issuance destroys that amount. **A change of substrate replaces the mechanism in this section and leaves Section 12 intact — which is the point of separating them.**

`rules/2026-01` defines no signed public history of settlement batches. The batch description each settlement carries (`batch_manifest`, [`settlement.binding/1`](../../../../../../ecosystem/settlement/settlement.binding/1/specification.md) §6) is unsigned and is accepted only where it repeats the anchored `batch_binding_preimage`: its authenticity comes from the anchor, not from a signature.

## 9. Settlement reference method

For `rules/2026-01`, `settlement_ref` resolution under `settlement.aggregated.xrpl/1` MUST use the exact tuple:

- `chain_id`
- `trx_id`
- `action_ordinal`
- `batch_id`
- `window_id`

That tuple MUST resolve to one and only one qualifying aggregated settlement container.

If the tuple resolves to zero or to more than one container under the same `xrpl_chain_id`, settlement-reference resolution MUST fail closed.

`settlement_ref` is serialized as exactly five colon-separated segments, in the order `chain_id:trx_id:action_ordinal:batch_id:window_id`. A verifier MUST reject any other segment count, and MUST bind each segment to the corresponding settlement-material field. The serialization is normative under `rules/2026-01`: it is the signed form the proof carries, so a verifier cannot recover the tuple without it.

## 10. Finality profile

For `rules/2026-01`, canonical settlement finality under `settlement.aggregated.xrpl/1` requires all of:

- the resolved action is included in block `block_num`
- `block_id` identifies that exact inclusion block
- `last_irreversible_block_num` is present
- `last_irreversible_block_id` is present
- `last_irreversible_block_num >= block_num`
- the declared finality context belongs to the same exact `xrpl_chain_id`

Any pending, provisional, soft-confirmed, or merely head-observed settlement context is non-conformant for `rules/2026-01`.

## 12. Capacity-consumption interpretation

For `rules/2026-01`, valid capacity consumption is established only by:

- the qualifying aggregated settlement action, of the exact transaction type `xrpl_action_name`, debited from `xrpl_settlement_account` and returning the `xrpl_mpt_issuance_id` MPT to its own issuance
- the exact claimed leaf inside that aggregated settlement container
- the exact settlement-bound `capacity_consumption_proof` required by `settlement-material/1`

Detached authorization, balances, cached availability, or operational accounting views MUST NOT be treated as equivalent.

---

## 13. Authorization-to-consumption bridge

For `rules/2026-01`, the authorization-to-consumption bridge is valid only if:

- `authorization_bridge_proof` resolves the consumed authorization uniquely under the same `system_id` and `rules_version`
- `authorization_bridge_proof.resolved_authorization_digest` equals `SHA3-256` of the Level-1 authorization binding the consumed capacity evidence proved
- that digest is deterministically derived from the resolved authorization artifact, not merely copied from the leaf context

The bridge carries no correlatable issuer scope: under the zero-data model the leaf binds neither an `issuer_scope` nor a `capacity_authorization_ref`, so the authorization is identified solely by `resolved_authorization_digest`.

If that derivation cannot be established, the claimed independence path MUST fail closed.

---

## 14. Leaf-assignment interpretation

For `rules/2026-01`, leaf assignment is valid only if:

- one exact `subtree_root` — the Merkle root over the commitments accepted into the leaf — is bound into one exact leaf
- one exact `leaf_index` and `sibling_hashes` yield one exact `settlement_root`
- one exact aggregated container interpretation results under `settlement.aggregated.xrpl/1`

Any context that permits multiple plausible leaf assignments is non-conformant.

## 15. Capacity-commitment TTL

For `rules/2026-01`, the capacity-commitment time-to-live is exactly:

- `commitment_ttl_seconds = 86400` (24 hours)

The commitment's expiry is derived as `expires_at = issued_at + commitment_ttl_seconds`. This TTL is global and public: it is fixed by this rules-domain instance, is identical for every issuer, and is **not** issuer-elected. An implementation MUST NOT substitute, default, or extend it, and MUST NOT use a longer contractual or business validity as the commitment's `expires_at`.

The TTL value is applied at **emission**, to derive the signed `expires_at`. Downstream surfaces (the submit path, the offline verifier) check that the signed `expires_at` equals `issued_at + commitment_ttl_seconds` under this entry, fail closed otherwise, and compare against that signed `expires_at`. Under `settlement.aggregated.xrpl/1`, the terminal temporal condition is anchored to the substrate: settlement is non-retroactive only if `finality_proof.block_timestamp <= expires_at` (materialized as the `SETTLEMENT_NOT_RETROACTIVE` check). A commitment whose settlement reaches irreversibility at a block later than `expires_at` MUST fail closed and can never reach `Independent` — the TTL is a hard deadline, not advisory.

The chosen 24-hour value gives an issuer ample headroom to emit and submit under any permitted close-window profile (§16), including the slowest, while bounding how long a signed-but-unconsumed commitment can sit before it must be resolved.

## 16. Close-window profiles

For `rules/2026-01`, the permitted settlement close-window profiles under `settlement.aggregated.xrpl/1` are exactly the following. The profile governs the **window close** and nothing else. Time-to-`Independent` also depends on settlement finality on the medium (§10), which the profile does not control and this entry does not quantify.

| `window_profile` | `max_window_seconds` | default |
|---|---:|:---:|
| `close_5s` | `5` | |
| `close_30s` | `30` | |
| `close_3min` | `180` | ✅ |
| `close_15min` | `900` | |
| `close_1h` | `3600` | |
| `close_3h` | `10800` | |
| `close_6h` | `21600` | |

- `close_3min` is the default. `close_5s` is the fastest permitted profile and `close_6h` the slowest; no other profile is permitted.
- The `window_profile` is elected by the issuer; a request that names no profile is assigned `close_3min`. Once assigned, the window's `close_due_at` is bounded and immutable — an implementation MUST NOT extend it.
- `allowed_fallbacks` MUST only degrade to a slower profile, never to a faster one.
- The slowest profile (`close_6h`) bounds how late a commitment can reach irreversibility: its `max_window_seconds` fits inside the capacity-commitment TTL of §14 (24 h), leaving headroom for emission→submit under any profile.
- This section defines only the window's structural timing. Any price associated with a profile is outside these public rules: it is not part of this registry entry and is never signed.

> **This entry states no latency figures, by design.** `window_profile` names and their `max_window_seconds` are normative and substrate-independent. Time-to-`Independent` is not: it adds settlement finality on the medium, which §10 defines structurally (`last_irreversible_block_num >= block_num`) and deliberately does not put in seconds. A figure here would be read as a service commitment. The bound this entry makes is the one in the table: `max_window_seconds`, issuer-elected, immutable once assigned.

## 17. Permitted subject profiles

For `rules/2026-01`, a certificate anchor subject MUST declare a `profile` drawn exactly from the following registered subject profiles. Each is governed by its own profile specification; this section enumerates which are admitted under `rules/2026-01`, exactly as §7 enumerates the single admitted settlement profile.

| `subjects[].profile` | governing specification |
|---|---|
| `subject.binary/1` | `profiles/subject.binary/1` |
| `subject.text/1` | `profiles/subject.text/1` |
| `subject.email/1` | `profiles/subject.email/1` |
| `subject.collection/1` | `profiles/subject.collection/1` |
| `subject.ai-decision/1` | `profiles/subject.ai-decision/1` |
| `subject.context-snapshot/1` | `profiles/subject.context-snapshot/1` |

- A subject whose declared `profile` is not one of the profiles above is non-conformant under `rules/2026-01`; a verifier MUST fail closed on it (it MUST NOT be treated as an opaque default or silently accepted).
- The same set binds every leaf of a `subject.collection/1` subject: each `leaves[].profile` MUST be one of the profiles above. A leaf that declares any other profile makes the certificate non-conformant, and a verifier that evaluates the collection's leaves MUST fail closed on it rather than report the profile as merely unsupported.
- The canonicalization and commitment rules of each subject are defined by its governing profile specification, not here. This section binds only the SET of admitted profiles.
- Resolution scope reminder (cf. the container/children boundary): a proof over any single subject binds that subject at its declared resolution and, for an opaque profile (e.g. `subject.binary/1`), asserts nothing about elements contained within it. Coverage of multiple elements individually requires a declared `subject.collection/1` with its `declared_cardinality`.
- Adding or removing an admitted subject profile after freeze is not an in-place modification; it is a successor rules-domain instance under [rules-domain/1/specification.md](../../../../../rules-domain/1/specification.md) Section 9.

## 18. Signature profiles

For `rules/2026-01`, each signed artifact is signed under exactly the profile below, by exactly the key named. The algorithms are those of [`composite-signature/1`](../../../../../composite-signature/1/specification.md) and NIST FIPS 204; the key material is the one each governing specification defines.

| signed artifact | signature profile | signing key |
|---|---|---|
| base certificate issuer signature ([`CCSP/1`](../../../../../../protocols/CCSP/1/specification.md)) | `certcrypt-composite-p256-mldsa65-v1` | the issuer's composite key carried in the signature |
| Level-1 capacity-authorization binding ([`capacity.evidence/1`](../../../../../../ecosystem/settlement/capacity.evidence/1/specification.md) `level1_signature`) | `certcrypt-composite-p256-mldsa65-v1` | the system-domain Level-1 capacity key of the bootstrap statement ([`system-foundation/1`](../../../../../system-foundation/1/specification.md) Section 6) |
| capacity-commitment holder signature ([`capacity.evidence/1`](../../../../../../ecosystem/settlement/capacity.evidence/1/specification.md) `commitment_signature_b64`) | ML-DSA-65 (pure) | the per-operation leaf key disclosed with its membership proof |
| independence proof signature ([`independence.proof/1`](../../../../../../models/independence.proof/1/specification.md)) | `certcrypt-composite-p256-mldsa65-v1` | the system-domain Level-1 capacity key of the bootstrap statement |

- The independence proof signature is **REQUIRED** under `rules/2026-01`. A proof that carries no signature, or whose signature does not verify under the profile and key above, MUST fail closed: the signature is what binds the proof's consumed commitments and settlement reference, and without it those members could be altered undetected.
- A verifier MUST take the expected profile from this section, not from the artifact. A signature that declares any other profile — including `certcrypt-composite-ed25519-mldsa65-v1`, which `composite-signature/1` registers but this rules domain does not admit — MUST fail closed, even where it would verify under that other profile.
- The signatures of the bootstrap statement itself are governed by [`system-foundation/1`](../../../../../system-foundation/1/specification.md) and the system manifest, not by this section.

## 19. Conformance

An implementation conforms to `rules/2026-01` if and only if:

- it carries `rules_version = "rules/2026-01"` exactly
- it satisfies the system-domain binding of Section 5
- it assigns fixation exclusively to the fixation service of Section 6
- it permits no settlement profile other than the one defined in Section 7
- it uses the exact settlement parameters of Section 8
- it resolves settlement references exactly as defined in Section 9
- it determines settlement finality exactly as defined in Section 10
- it uses exactly the settlement-material contract of Section 11
- it interprets capacity consumption exactly as defined in Section 12
- it validates the authorization-to-consumption bridge exactly as defined in Section 13
- it interprets leaf assignment exactly as defined in Section 14
- it applies exactly the capacity-commitment TTL of Section 15
- it uses only the close-window profiles of Section 16, with the default and fallback rules declared there
- it admits only the subject profiles of Section 17
- it signs and verifies each artifact exactly under the signature profile and key of Section 18

Partial implementations MUST NOT claim full conformance to `rules/2026-01`.

## 20. Failure conditions

Validation under `rules/2026-01` MUST fail if any of the following hold:

- the artifact carries a `rules_version` other than `"rules/2026-01"`
- the system-domain binding of Section 5 does not hold
- members of a submission set are accepted or rejected by a party other than the fixation service of Section 6
- the artifact claims a settlement profile other than `settlement.aggregated.xrpl/1`
- any of the settlement parameters of Section 8 are absent, defaulted, or differ from the values declared there
- `xrpl_settlement_account` is unset or empty, or the settlement's originating account is not exactly `xrpl_settlement_account`
- the `settlement_ref` does not resolve to exactly one qualifying aggregated settlement container
- finality conditions of Section 10 are not all satisfied
- a settlement-material contract other than the one declared in Section 11 is used
- capacity consumption is claimed via any path other than the one defined in Section 12
- the authorization-to-consumption bridge of Section 13 cannot be established
- leaf assignment of Section 14 is ambiguous or non-unique
- a commitment's `expires_at` differs from `issued_at + commitment_ttl_seconds` of Section 15, or its settlement's `finality_proof.block_timestamp` is later than `expires_at`
- a settlement window uses a close-window profile not listed in Section 16, extends its `close_due_at`, or falls back to a faster profile
- a subject, or a leaf of a `subject.collection/1` subject, declares a `profile` not admitted by Section 17
- an independence proof carries no signature, or any signature of Section 18 declares a profile other than the one admitted there or does not verify under the key named there

No partial success state MAY substitute for a failed required condition.

## 21. Immutability and successor rules domains

This registry entry is frozen: its parameters MUST NOT change in place under any
circumstance.

A successor rules-domain instance is registered as a new entry in the same registry. It does not modify `rules/2026-01`. Equivalence, compatibility, and successor relationships between `rules/2026-01` and any other rules-domain instance are governed by [rules-domain/1/specification.md](../../../../../rules-domain/1/specification.md) Section 9.

After freeze, any change of a binding identifier (for example, a different `xrpl_chain_id`, `xrpl_mpt_issuance_id`, `xrpl_settlement_account`, or `xrpl_action_name`) is not permitted as an in-place modification of `rules/2026-01`. Such a change MUST be expressed as a new registry entry under a new `rules_version` identifier.
